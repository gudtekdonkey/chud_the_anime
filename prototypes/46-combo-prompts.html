Prototype 46 · combat input

Combo prompts and swipe moves

Play it. Hit a samurai with J (or tap the right half on a phone) and the next link of the chain shows as a prompt over him: an arrow, with a ring closing on the beat. Answer it in time and the next cut plays; each direction is a different cut. On a phone the same prompt is answered with a swipe that way. These are the game's own modules: the ronin, his cuts, the black slash, the samurai's hits and reactions.

This uses today's rig and side-on attacks on today's floor. The new 3/4 camera and the Iron Ash art come later; the input design carries over to them unchanged.

    What each answer does

    A direction means the same on every screen: → is always toward the enemy (the arrow drawn on screen points at him, so it flips when he is on your left). Every link is one of the J ladder's own cuts from player/combo.js, picked by the answer instead of by count, so timing, hits, the black slash and the hit pause are the game's.

    Timing: the white ring closes on the beat (the prompt lead after the hit). Within 0.05 s of it is PERFECT (a little Qi and a burst of sparks), before it or in the first 60% of the window after it is GOOD, the rest is LATE (still a hit). After the window closes it is a MISS. A press that comes before the current cut's chain beat (0.3 s) is held until that beat, as J is today, so the cuts always flow out of each other's follow-through and never cut off a swing.

    Reading a swipe

    Every stroke on the right side is read when the finger lifts (a hold and a double tap are read while it is down). All thresholds are CSS pixels and milliseconds, the same on every phone, and all of them are on the panel.

    How it fits the game

    Dead Cells flow

    Dead Cells feels fluid because every input is buffered and lands on the next allowed beat, and because direction and attack are one decision. Here the prompt is that decision: the answer is read at once, held to the current cut's chain beat (0.3 s, the same GO the J ladder uses) and played from the follow-through. Nothing pauses unless slow motion is switched on. A prompt only appears after a landed cut, so a whiff ends the chain on its own, with no extra rule.

    The J ladder

    The six cuts in CUTS stay exactly as they are. Today J plays them in order; with prompts the answer picks which one comes next, and the last link is always J6's flash step. comboMax() keeps its job: basic skill (landed cuts: 2 links to start, 3 at 40, 4 at 120, 5 at 250, 6 at 450) sets how long a chain may run. Free mode on the panel is today's game.

    Flow

    Every answered link is a landed basic cut, so landCut() counts it unchanged: six in a row within 1.6 s still earn Flow for 8 s. Proposed: a PERFECT also keeps the Flow gap open for its full 1.6 s again, so a clean chain carries Flow from one fight into the next.

    Executions (K)

    When the finisher lands and a lone samurai is in K's reach, the last prompt is K: the chain ends in an execution, and K's chaining rules take it from there. The stretch, not built: one prompt inside an execution, on its killing beat, for the bloodier ending (it needs a hook in assassin/).

    Growth

    Basic skill already lengthens the chain. The skill trees can widen what a chain offers: once a skill is mastered its key can appear as a link (an I prompt casts the glitch double slash inside the chain, off cooldown, the way Flow does), and a tree's fork can trade a longer window for a harder prompt. Power I, II and III could add the diagonals as extra prompts.

    Accessibility

    Auto plays every link on the beat; Hold to continue answers each prompt while J (or a finger) is held; Generous widens the window by half and lets a wrong answer stand without ending the chain; slow motion is separate. The prompt never relies on colour: an arrow, a letter or a dot, a double chevron and a white frame for the finisher, a red frame and K for the execution, and the grade as a word. Reduced motion turns off the screen shake.
