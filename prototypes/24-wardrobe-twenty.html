The forty

Each figure wears one piece alone and loops through a breath, a run, a cut and the counter stance, grouped by slot. The first twenty are the earlier set; the twenty marked new fill out every slot: torn hakama, tight trousers, shin guards, short kimono, jinbaori, chain shirt, monk's robe, split cape, sashimono banner, travel bundle, sake gourd, belt and pouches, tassets, shoulder strap, prayer beads, fur mantle, kataginu, neckerchief, iron gorget and hand guards.

Armour dyes

Armour can take a dark colour: plates, mail, shin guards, tassets, the gorget, sleeves and hand guards, and the iron hats. Robes and other cloth stay black. A dye tints each black to its hue at the same lightness, so the armour stays dark and still reads as part of his silhouette. Here is the same armour in each dye; try them on in the fitting room.

Hats and hair

The hat is an item now, not part of him, so he can go bareheaded. Hair sits on his scalp, and long styles have cloth tails that swing. Under a hat, whatever is above the brim hides, and the sides and tails still show. Twenty-two styles. The rare bandana is the one headwear in any colour, and it only fits styles with nothing on the crown: knots, buns, crests and volume don't go under it. Tick a box to see every style under the straw hat or the bandana.

Masks

Ten masks in the face slot. Unlike the clothes they come in any colour, so they are the one place his silhouette can carry a colour of its own. Each is drawn on the side of his face under the hat brim, and his cyan eye shows through all but the visor. Most fit under a hat. The oni's horns and the kitsune's ears don't, except in their special pairs below, and each hat has its own rule. Shuffle for random colours, or wear one in the fitting room.

Hats and masks together

Each hat says which masks fit under it. Most take any mask that has no horns or ears. The tengai basket covers his whole face, so it takes none. The kabuto takes only the menpo. Four pairs do something of their own. Click any cell to try that pair in the fitting room.

The layering system

Each slot is a layer, and he wears one piece per slot. Every pixel of him and his clothes carries a depth, and the nearer one wins. A piece's depth is where its cloth really sits, plus its layer's small nudge. The nudge only settles ties, such as a sash and a coat at the same radius. It never lifts a cape out from behind him.

Build-up of the fitting-room outfit

Around his body

The far arm and far leg sit behind everything. The torso is the zero plane. Clothes wrap round it, so the half of a sash or mantle behind him hides and the near half shows.

The near arm and the blade

In the side view his near arm is drawn over whatever he wears, as it always was. Wraps and armoured sleeves ride on that arm. A held blade sits above everything.

Cloth

Loose parts are cloth pinned to the bones. They fall under gravity and catch the breeze and his own speed, and they are kept out of his body and legs. Stiff pieces pull back to their cut.

Turning and other views

Each piece is measured from the skeleton, not drawn in pixels. When the port system brings the front and back views, only the skeleton changes and all forty follow.

The palette

Seven shades of black for the clothes, nothing brighter. The accents come from the effects, his eyes and the masks, which can be any colour.

Clothing slots: recommended, one per layer

Each slot is a layer, and he wears at most one piece in each: legs, body, back, waist, ties, shoulders, neck and hands for clothes, then hair, face (masks) and head (hats). That gives him up to eleven pieces and never two mantles. On the head the order is scalp, hair, mask, hat.

Why slots

Two pieces in the same place would fight for the same pixels, like two mantles or a cape inside a cape. One per slot rules that out, so any outfit a player builds reads cleanly.

Why slot = layer

The slot tells you the layer, so the stacking order never needs tuning outfit by outfit. A new piece only has to pick its slot to layer correctly.

What slots give the game

Drops and loot can name a slot ("a new waist piece"). Each slot can carry one kind of bonus later, such as armour on the body or speed on the legs, and the wardrobe screen is just eight rows.

Not recommended

Free stacking with no slots looks messy at 20 px tall and needs a rule for every pair. Fewer slots, such as merging waist and ties, would stop the Pilgrim wearing a sash and a tasuki together.

One cross-slot rule: each hat decides which masks it takes (see Hats and masks together). When the two don't go together, whichever went on last stays.

  6. Armour dyes. Are these seven the right kind of dark? Should a dye be found, bought, or picked freely?
  7. Masks. Which of the ten stay, and should their colours be random per drop, dyed by the player, or fixed?
  8. The layer order. For example, should the sash go over the coat (as now) or under it?
